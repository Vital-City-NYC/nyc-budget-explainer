## Bottom line, with the best quotes

1. Comptroller, *Comments on NYC's FY2027 Adopted Budget*, Aug 12, 2026, p.24 (local PDF): https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/Comments-on-New-York-Citys-Fiscal-Year-2027-Adopted-Budget.pdf
   > "The Adopted Budget for FY 2027 totals $125.84 billion. Just over a quarter of the total budget ($34.11 billion or 27.1 percent) is allocated for education spending, which includes funding the Department of Education (DOE) and the City University of New York (CUNY), followed by $23.37 billion for the City's social service agencies (18.6 percent). Other agency spending comprises $35.03 billion (27.8 percent of the budget). Spending on fringe benefits and pensions for City employees and retirees account for another $24.36 billion (19.4 percent of the budget). Debt service costs to pay for the City's capital program account for $7.37 billion (5.9 percent). Budgeted payments for judgments and claims comprise $1.15 billion (0.9 percent)."
   The Comptroller calls these "centrally budgeted expenditures, such as fringe benefits, debt service, and pension contributions" (p.24). Neither the Comptroller nor IBO uses the phrase "non-discretionary" or "fixed costs" in the 2026 documents read here; that framing is ours. Derived from the Comptroller's Table 13 (p.45, adjusted for prepayments): pensions $8,707M + health insurance $10,862M + other fringe $4,797M + Medicaid $6,790M + judgments and claims $1,148M + debt service $9,327M = $41.6B, or 32.6 percent of $127,751M adjusted expenditures (28.8 percent without other fringe). Using the p.24 shares: fringe and pensions 19.4 + debt service 5.9 + judgments 0.9 + Medicaid 5.4 = about 31.6 percent of the $125.84B budget. **Label this as derived, not quoted.**
2. The growth problem is in these lines. Comptroller p.46 (local PDF): "As shown in Table 15, most of the growth in spending over the Plan period comes from PS-related costs, including salaries and wages, pension, and health insurance. PS costs are budgeted to grow at an annual average rate of 2.7 percent while OTPS costs are budgeted to grow by 1.0 percent from FY 2027 to FY 2030. Debt service costs are projected to grow at an annual average rate of 10.2 percent."
3. The mayor can order cuts only in the discretionary remainder. Comptroller p.8: "Following budget adoption, the administration announced, in late July, 2.5 percent annual savings targets for City agencies in FY 2027 through FY 2030. ... This Office preliminarily estimates such a reduction in City-funded spending could save approximately $1.50 billion annually with the actual impact dependent upon exemptions and if savings targets are applied to centralized costs."

## Gaps and reserves (Comptroller FY27 Adopted, local PDF)

- p.46, CONFIRMED verbatim: "While the FY 2027 budget is, as required by law, balanced, the June 2026 Plan presents budget gaps of $6.44 billion in FY 2028, $8.21 billion in FY 2029, and $8.52 billion in FY 2030."
- p.24: "The Adopted Budget also includes a general reserve of $450 million, which accounts for 0.4 percent of the budget. ... In the Preliminary Budget released in February, the administration lowered the FY 2027 General Reserve from $1.2 billion to its statutory minimum of $100 million. The FY 2027 Capital Stabilization Reserve (another in-year reserve) budgeted for $250 million was eliminated. After the $350 million partial restoration of the General Reserve, FY 2027 in-year reserves stand $1.0 billion below the $1.45 billion amount typically available at the start of the fiscal year."
- p.57: "In addition to the budgeted reserve funds, the City holds long-term reserves in its rainy-day fund, the Revenue Stabilization Fund (RSF), and the Retiree Health Benefit Trust (RHBT). The RSF's current balance is $1.97 billion. The RHBT held a balance of $5.22 billion as of the end of FY 2025." and "Currently, RSF has no target balance, no rules governing the timing or size of deposits, no replenishment mechanism after use, and only loose guidelines for withdrawals." p.58: "No deposit took place in FY 2026, and none is planned for FY 2027."
- p.7, the one-shots: "the Mamdani administration relied on $6.07 billion in temporary measures announced over the course of the budget cycle to close its projected budget gaps in FY 2026 and FY 2027."
